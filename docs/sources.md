# Sources

The published works the documents rest on, by first author and year as the text cites them. Each says what is taken from it and where.

- **Beust 2003.** H. Beust. Symplectic integration of hierarchical stellar systems. Astronomy and Astrophysics 400, 1129. The mapping of Wisdom and Holman carried to a tree of nested orbits, in the integrator of `architecture.md`, Stability.
- **Brown and Butler 2023.** M. E. Brown, B. J. Butler. Masses and densities of dwarf planet satellites measured with ALMA. The Planetary Science Journal 4, 193. [arXiv:2307.04848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04848). The mass of Vanth, 16 percent of Orcus: the double planets of `nature.md`.
- **Broyden 1965.** C. G. Broyden. A class of methods for solving nonlinear simultaneous equations. Mathematics of Computation 19, 577. The update of the slopes when a push is corrected, `architecture.md`, Flight.
- **Collier Cameron and Jardine 2018.** A. Collier Cameron, M. Jardine. Hierarchical Bayesian calibration of tidal orbit decay rates among hot Jupiters. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 476, 2542. [arXiv:1801.10561](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10561). One end of the quality factor of the tide a planet raises on its star, `nature.md`.
- **Domingos and others 2006.** R. C. Domingos, O. C. Winter, T. Yokoyama. Stable satellites around extrasolar giant planets. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 373, 1227. The edge of stable moons at 0.49 of the hold of a planet, beyond which a moon of a sinking planet is torn away, `nature.md`.
- **Eggleton 1983.** P. P. Eggleton. Approximations to the radii of Roche lobes. The Astrophysical Journal 268, 368. The room a star has in a close pair, `nature.md`.
- **Fehlberg 1968.** E. Fehlberg. Classical fifth-, sixth-, seventh-, and eighth-order Runge-Kutta formulas with stepsize control. NASA Technical Report R-287. The embedded pair of orders 7 and 8 that flies the ship, `architecture.md`, Flight.
- **Hamer and Schlaufman 2019.** J. H. Hamer, K. C. Schlaufman. Hot Jupiters are destroyed by tides while their host stars are on the main sequence. The Astronomical Journal 158, 190. [arXiv:1908.06998](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06998). The other end of the quality factor of the tide on the star, `nature.md`.
- **Hayashi 1961.** C. Hayashi. Stellar evolution in early phases of gravitational contraction. Publications of the Astronomical Society of Japan 13, 450. The track of a young star before the main sequence, `nature.md`.
- **Hjellming and Webbink 1987.** M. S. Hjellming, R. F. Webbink. Thresholds for rapid mass transfer in binary systems. I. Polytropic models. The Astrophysical Journal 318, 794. The older limit of stable transfer from a giant, set aside in `nature.md`.
- **Holman and Wiegert 1999.** M. J. Holman, P. A. Wiegert. Long-term stability of planets in binary systems. The Astronomical Journal 117, 621. How far orbits last around one star of a pair and from how far around both, `nature.md`.
- **Hurley and others 2000.** J. R. Hurley, O. R. Pols, C. A. Tout. Comprehensive analytic formulae for stellar evolution as a function of mass and metallicity. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 315, 543. The radius of a red star from its light and mass, `nature.md`.
- **Hut 1981.** P. Hut. Tidal evolution in close binary systems. Astronomy and Astrophysics 99, 126. The factors for an oval orbit under the tide of a convective envelope, `nature.md`.
- **Jackson and others 2008.** B. Jackson, R. Greenberg, R. Barnes. Tidal evolution of close-in extrasolar planets. The Astrophysical Journal 678, 1396. [arXiv:0802.1543](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1543). The factors for an oval orbit under the tide a planet raises on its star, `nature.md`.
- **Kiss and others 2025.** C. Kiss and others. Abstract EPSC-DPS2025-905, EPSC-DPS Joint Meeting 2025. [Abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EPSC-DPS2025/EPSC-DPS2025-905.html). The masses of Ilmarë and Actaea against Varda and Salacia: the double planets of `nature.md`.
- **Mardling and Aarseth 2001.** R. A. Mardling, S. J. Aarseth. Tidal interactions in star cluster simulations. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 321, 398. When a group inside a wider orbit holds together, `nature.md`.
- **Mestel 1952.** L. Mestel. On the theory of white dwarf stars. I. The energy sources of white dwarfs. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 112, 583. How a white dwarf cools, `nature.md`.
- **Nakajima and others 2022.** M. Nakajima, H. Genda, E. Asphaug, S. Ida. Large planets may not form fractionally large moons. Nature Communications 13. [doi:10.1038/s41467-022-28063-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28063-8). No large moon from an impact on a rocky planet above six Earths or an icy one above one, `nature.md`.
- **Namouni 2010.** F. Namouni. The fate of moons of close-in giant exoplanets. The Astrophysical Journal 719, L145. Moons of a planet that moves inward are torn away, `nature.md`.
- **Nelemans and others 2025.** G. Nelemans, H. P. Preece, K. D. Temmink, J. Munday, O. R. Pols. The first phase of mass transfer in low-mass binaries: neither stable nor a common envelope. Astronomy and Astrophysics 700, A219. [arXiv:2506.16832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16832). The measured double white dwarfs fit neither limit of stable transfer well, `nature.md`.
- **Pavlovskii and Ivanova 2015.** K. Pavlovskii, N. Ivanova. Mass transfer from giant donors. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 449, 4415. The range of the ratio of masses under which transfer from a giant stays stable, `nature.md`.
- **Tauris and Savonije 1999.** T. M. Tauris, G. J. Savonije. Formation of millisecond pulsars. I. Evolution of low-mass X-ray binaries with Porb > 2 days. Astronomy and Astrophysics 350, 928. The mass of a white dwarf left by stable transfer against the period of its orbit, a check in `nature.md`.
- **Temmink and others 2023.** K. D. Temmink, O. R. Pols, S. Justham, A. G. Istrate, S. Toonen. Coping with loss: stability of mass transfer from post-main-sequence donor stars. Astronomy and Astrophysics 669, A45. [arXiv:2209.12707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12707). How the ratio of masses for stable transfer changes along the giant branch, `nature.md`.
- **Villaver and Livio 2009.** E. Villaver, M. Livio. The orbital evolution of gas giant planets around giant stars. The Astrophysical Journal 705, L81. The tide of a convective envelope after Zahn, and how far a giant draws in its planets, `nature.md`.
- **Wisdom and Holman 1991.** J. Wisdom, M. Holman. Symplectic maps for the n-body problem. The Astronomical Journal 102, 1528. The mapping the integrator steps by, `architecture.md`, Stability.
- **Zahn 1977.** J.-P. Zahn. Tidal friction in close binary stars. Astronomy and Astrophysics 57, 383. The tide of a convective envelope, `nature.md`.
